# PART I

## Item 1. Business

### Overview

Halvorsen Instruments, Inc. ("Halvorsen," the "Company," "we," "us" or "our") designs, manufactures and services precision flow measurement and process analysis instruments used by water utilities, chemical and pharmaceutical producers, food and beverage processors, and oil and gas operators. Founded in 1958 as a maker of mechanical flow gauges, we now derive approximately 71% of our net sales from electronic and ultrasonic instruments, related software and recurring calibration and maintenance services. We completed our initial public offering in 2016 and operate on a 52- or 53-week fiscal year ending on the last Saturday of September. Fiscal 2025 was a 52-week year.

### Segments

We report results in two segments.

**Process Measurement.** This segment, which generated $561.9 million, or 66.7%, of fiscal 2025 net sales, sells inline ultrasonic and electromagnetic flow meters, pressure transmitters and level sensors. Customers include municipal water systems, which accounted for 24% of segment sales, and pharmaceutical manufacturers, which accounted for 19%.

**Analytical and Services.** This segment generated $280.4 million, or 33.3%, of net sales and consists of laboratory and online analyzers for conductivity, turbidity and dissolved oxygen, together with our field calibration, repair and remote-monitoring subscriptions. Services and subscription revenue represented 58% of the segment's sales in fiscal 2025, compared with 55% in fiscal 2024.

### Strategy

Our strategy rests on four priorities: (1) expanding our installed base of connected instruments, which now exceeds 410,000 devices; (2) growing recurring service and software revenue; (3) selective acquisitions of complementary sensor and analytics businesses; and (4) improving operating margin through manufacturing consolidation. In fiscal 2025 we began a restructuring program to close our Duluth, Minnesota assembly facility and transfer production to our plants in Rochester and Monterrey, Mexico.

### Customers, Sales and Distribution

We sell through a direct sales force of 214 employees and through approximately 160 independent distributors and representatives in 38 countries. No single customer accounted for more than 10% of net sales in fiscal 2025, 2024 or 2023. Sales outside the United States represented 39% of net sales in fiscal 2025. Our order backlog at September 27, 2025 was $188.2 million, compared with $171.5 million a year earlier, and we expect to ship approximately 86% of it within the next twelve months.

### Competition

The markets for our products are fragmented. We compete with large diversified automation companies and with specialized instrument makers on the basis of measurement accuracy, reliability, breadth of certifications, installed-base service coverage and price. Several competitors have greater financial resources than we do.

### Research and Development

We spent $58.9 million, $55.1 million and $51.6 million on research and development in fiscal 2025, 2024 and 2023, respectively. At year end, 276 of our employees worked in engineering roles, principally in Rochester and in our software center in Austin, Texas.

### Raw Materials and Manufacturing

Principal inputs include stainless steel and specialty alloys, piezoelectric ceramics, printed circuit assemblies and semiconductors. We source certain microcontrollers from a single supplier. Although lead times have normalized from their peak in fiscal 2022, a shortage could delay shipments.

### Intellectual Property

We held 187 issued United States patents and 142 foreign patents at year end, with expiration dates ranging from 2026 to 2044. We do not consider any single patent or license to be material to our business as a whole.

### Human Capital

As of September 27, 2025 we employed 3,041 people, of whom 1,198 were located outside the United States. Approximately 9% of our employees are covered by collective bargaining agreements, principally in Monterrey. Voluntary turnover was 8.4% in fiscal 2025.

### Regulation and Available Information

Our products are subject to product safety, electromagnetic compatibility, hazardous-area and export control regulations, as well as environmental laws governing the use of restricted substances. Our reports on Forms 10-K, 10-Q and 8-K, and amendments to them, are available free of charge on our website, www.halvorsen-instruments.example, as soon as reasonably practicable after we file them with the SEC.

## Item 1A. Risk Factors

The following risks could materially affect our business, financial condition and results of operations.

**Our results depend on capital spending by municipal and industrial customers.** Demand for our process instruments is tied to infrastructure budgets, plant expansions and maintenance cycles. A decline in municipal funding or a deferral of industrial projects could reduce orders. In fiscal 2025, orders from oil and gas customers decreased 11% from the prior year.

**We have significant indebtedness.** At September 27, 2025 we had $246.0 million of outstanding borrowings under our credit agreement. Our agreement contains covenants that limit additional indebtedness, liens and acquisitions and requires us to maintain a total leverage ratio not exceeding 3.50 to 1.00. Borrowings bear interest at variable rates, so a 100 basis point increase in rates would increase annual interest expense by approximately $2.5 million.

**Our restructuring program may not deliver the expected savings.** We expect to incur total charges of approximately $11 million through fiscal 2027 in connection with the closure of the Duluth facility and anticipate annualized savings of $9 million to $11 million once complete. Delays in transferring production could disrupt deliveries.

**We rely on a limited number of suppliers for certain components.** The loss of a sole-source supplier or a significant increase in component prices could harm our margins.

**Tariffs and trade restrictions could increase our costs.** We import components into the United States and export finished instruments from our Monterrey plant. Changes in tariffs, customs rules or export controls could raise costs or restrict sales.

**Our software and connected products are exposed to cybersecurity threats.** A breach of our remote-monitoring platform could expose customer data and expose us to claims.

**We may be unable to integrate acquisitions.** We acquired Tavistock Sensor Systems in fiscal 2022 and may pursue additional transactions.

**Goodwill and intangible assets could become impaired.** Goodwill of $214.3 million represented 25% of our total assets at year end. An impairment charge would reduce earnings.

## Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Our Audit Committee oversees cybersecurity risk. Our Vice President of Information Security, who has more than 20 years of experience in the field, reports quarterly to the committee on threat trends, incidents and testing results. We maintain an incident response plan, conduct annual third-party penetration tests and require annual training for all employees. We did not identify any cybersecurity incident during fiscal 2025 that materially affected us.

## Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

### Results of Operations

**Net sales.** Net sales increased $50.7 million, or 6.4%, to $842.3 million. Process Measurement sales rose 5.1% on higher pricing of approximately 3 points and strong demand from pharmaceutical and municipal customers, partly offset by lower oil and gas volumes. Analytical and Services sales rose 9.2%, led by growth in remote-monitoring subscriptions and field calibration contracts. Foreign currency translation reduced net sales by approximately $4.1 million.

**Gross profit.** Gross margin expanded 100 basis points to 40.0% because of favorable price realization, a higher mix of services and lower freight costs, partly offset by wage inflation at our Monterrey plant.

**Operating expenses.** Research and development expense rose 6.9% to $58.9 million. Selling, general and administrative expense increased 5.9% to $187.3 million, principally from higher commissions and incentive compensation. Amortization of intangibles was $12.4 million. We recorded restructuring charges of $4.1 million related to severance and asset write-downs at Duluth.

**Interest and taxes.** Interest expense decreased to $14.6 million from $15.8 million as a result of $27.0 million of debt repayments. Our effective tax rate was 20.7% compared with 21.1% in fiscal 2024, reflecting research credits and the vesting of share-based awards.

### Liquidity and Capital Resources

Cash provided by operating activities was $91.4 million in fiscal 2025 compared with $72.5 million in fiscal 2024, driven by higher earnings and a smaller build in inventories. Capital expenditures were $24.8 million. We used cash to repay $27.0 million of term loan borrowings and to repurchase 288,000 shares of our common stock for $10.0 million. At year end we held $61.8 million of cash and had $154.0 million available under our $200.0 million revolving credit facility. We believe cash on hand, cash from operations and availability under our credit agreement will be sufficient for at least the next twelve months.

### Critical Accounting Estimates

Our most significant estimates relate to revenue recognition for multi-element arrangements, inventory valuation, the valuation of goodwill and acquired intangible assets, and income taxes. We perform our annual goodwill impairment test as of the first day of the fourth fiscal quarter. The fair value of each reporting unit exceeded its carrying value by more than 30% in fiscal 2025.

## Item 7A. Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk

We are exposed to changes in interest rates on our variable-rate debt and to foreign currency fluctuations, principally the euro and the Mexican peso. A hypothetical 10% strengthening of the U.S. dollar would have reduced fiscal 2025 operating income by approximately $3.2 million. We do not use derivative instruments for speculative purposes.

### Note 1. Summary of Significant Accounting Policies

The consolidated financial statements include the accounts of Halvorsen Instruments, Inc. and its wholly owned subsidiaries. All intercompany balances are eliminated. Revenue from instrument sales is recognized when control transfers to the customer, generally upon shipment. Revenue from calibration contracts and monitoring subscriptions is recognized ratably over the service period. Inventories are stated at the lower of cost (first-in, first-out) or net realizable value.
