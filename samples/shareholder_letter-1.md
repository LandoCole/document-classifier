PINECREST BANCORP, INC.

2025 ANNUAL REPORT

To Our Shareholders:

When I wrote to you a year ago, I said that 2025 would be a year of proving what we had built. I am pleased to report that, on almost every measure we care about, we did.

Pinecrest earned $182.4 million in 2025, or $3.41 per diluted share, compared with $151.9 million, or $2.83 per share, in 2024. Return on average tangible common equity rose to 15.2 percent from 12.8 percent. Net interest income grew 7 percent to $611 million as our deposit costs stabilized and we repriced roughly $2.3 billion of fixed-rate loans. Total assets ended the year at $19.8 billion, loans at $13.1 billion and deposits at $16.2 billion. Our common equity tier 1 capital ratio was 11.4 percent, well above the regulatory minimum.

I know that many of you read the numbers first, and the full financial statements are in the enclosed Form 10-K, which we filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission on February 26. But numbers do not capture why this year mattered, so let me tell you what I think does.

## A bank is a promise kept

In March, a regional competitor in the Carolinas failed over a single weekend. Our branch managers in Greensboro and Charlotte spent the following Monday opening accounts for business owners who could not get a payroll processed. We did not advertise. We simply answered the phone. Deposits from that market grew $740 million in the second quarter, and, more important, we kept nearly all of them. That is what trust looks like in this business.

## Credit quality

Our net charge-offs were 0.21 percent of average loans, and nonperforming assets declined to 0.48 percent of total assets. We continue to be cautious about commercial real estate. Office exposure is now 3.9 percent of our loan book, and we have taken $41 million of reserves against it. Our allowance for credit losses stands at 1.34 percent of loans. We would rather explain a conservative reserve than a surprise.

## Investing in the franchise

We opened eleven branches and closed nine, concentrating on growth markets in Tennessee, Georgia and North Carolina. We completed the integration of Harlan Valley Trust, acquired in 2024, ahead of schedule, and we have realized $38 million of the $45 million in annual cost savings we promised. Our new commercial banking platform went live in October, and early results suggest we can onboard a business client in four days rather than twenty.

## Returning capital

Your Board raised the quarterly dividend 9 percent to $0.48 per share in the fourth quarter, our twelfth consecutive annual increase. We also repurchased 3.2 million shares for $118 million. At year-end the Board authorized a new repurchase program of up to $200 million. We intend to continue to balance dividends, buybacks and growth, with growth first.

## Governance and our people

I want to thank Eleanor Whitcomb, who retires from our Board at the Annual Meeting after eighteen years of service, and welcome Marcus Delgado, formerly chief risk officer of a large insurer, who joins as a nominee. You will find details about the nominees, executive compensation and the other items to be voted on in the proxy statement that accompanies this report. I encourage you to vote your shares, by internet, phone or mail, whether or not you plan to attend our meeting on April 23 in Knoxville.

Our 4,860 employees are the reason for these results. In a year in which many companies cut back, we raised our minimum wage to $22 an hour and expanded our tuition program to part-time staff. I believe this is not charity. It is how you build a bank customers recommend to their friends.

## Looking ahead

I will not predict interest rates, and neither should you. We have positioned the balance sheet to perform reasonably in a range of outcomes. Our priorities for 2026 are straightforward: grow core deposits, hold the line on expenses, keep credit disciplined and finish the technology work we started. If we do those things well, the earnings will follow.

Thank you for your investment in Pinecrest and for the confidence you have placed in us.

Sincerely,

Harold J. Brennan
Chairman and Chief Executive Officer
