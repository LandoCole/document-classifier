DUNMORE TOOL & DIE COMPANY
1180 Foundry Road
Erie, Pennsylvania 16503

April 2026

Dear Fellow Shareholders,

My grandfather started this company in a rented garage with two lathes and a handshake deal with a local pump maker. I think about that garage every spring when I sit down to write this letter, because the questions he asked are still the right ones. Do we make something customers need? Do we make it well? Are we honest about the price?

Last year was a hard one, and I won't dress it up. Sales fell to $87.3 million from $96.1 million. Two of our largest automotive customers delayed programs and we ran the east plant on a four-day week from June through September. Net income was $2.1 million, or $0.58 per share, down from $5.4 million. We did not lay anyone off. That was a choice, and I would make it again, though I know it cost you something.

Here is what went right.

First, our aerospace and medical work grew 22 percent and is now 31 percent of sales, up from 24 percent. These contracts are smaller and more demanding but they pay better and they don't swing with the car cycle.

Second, we paid off the last of the 2019 term loan. We now carry $4.6 million of debt against $11.9 million of cash. The balance sheet is the strongest it has been in my thirty-one years here.

Third, we bought two five-axis machining centers, which we expect to be fully used by autumn.

On the dividend: the Board maintained the quarterly payment of $0.15 per share. I do not expect to cut it. I also will not promise to raise it until I see demand come back.

A word about the Annual Meeting. It will be held on May 21 at the Erie Club at 10:00 a.m. The notice and proxy materials were mailed to you this month. There are only three items: election of seven directors, ratification of our auditors and an advisory vote on executive pay. I would be grateful if you voted, and even more grateful if you came and told me what you think. Lunch is on us.

You will also find our Annual Report on Form 10-K enclosed. It has the audited numbers and the long list of risks that our lawyers insist on. I would only add one risk of my own: that we get complacent.

Thank you for your patience and your trust.

Sincerely,

William R. Dunmore III
Chairman of the Board and President
