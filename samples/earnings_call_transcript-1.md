# Northquay Semiconductor Corp. (NQSC) Q2 2026 Earnings Call

### Company Participants

Good afternoon, and welcome to the Northquay Semiconductor second quarter fiscal 2026 earnings conference call. All participants are in listen-only mode. After the speakers' remarks, there will be a question-and-answer session. [Operator Instructions] As a reminder, this conference call is being recorded. I would now like to turn the call over to Alexandra Whitcomb, Vice President of Investor Relations. Please go ahead.

## Alexandra Whitcomb

Thank you, operator, and good afternoon, everyone. With me today are Jamal Idowu, our CEO, and Sung-Min Park, our CFO. Our earnings press release and the slides that accompany this call are available on the Investor Relations section of our website.

During this call we will make forward-looking statements, including statements about our expected revenue, margins and product launches. These statements are subject to risks and uncertainties that could cause actual results to differ materially, including those described in our most recent Annual Report on Form 10-K and in the press release we issued earlier today. We assume no obligation to update them. We will also discuss non-GAAP financial measures; reconciliations to the most directly comparable GAAP measures are in our press release. With that, I'll turn the call over to Jamal.

## Jamal R. Idowu

Thanks, Alexandra, and thank you all for joining us. We delivered a strong second quarter. Revenue was $684 million, up 17% year over year and above the high end of our guidance range. Non-GAAP earnings per share were $1.38, up 26%.

Let me start with the three themes that defined the quarter. First, our automotive and industrial segment grew 24% year over year, reaching a record $312 million. We saw broad-based strength in power management and our new sensing products, which are now designed into 19 vehicle platforms. Second, our data center interconnect business grew 31%, and we began shipping our 1.6-terabit optical driver to two hyperscale customers. Third, we continued to invest through the cycle. R&D spending was 19% of revenue, and we taped out two chips on a leading-edge process node.

Consumer and mobile were softer, down 6% year over year, in line with our expectations, as customers worked through inventory. We believe that inventory correction is largely behind us and expect that segment to return to growth in the fourth quarter. I'd also note that we've seen no change in our relationships with our key foundry partners, and we expect adequate capacity to support our second half ramp. Now I'll hand it over to Sung-Min to walk through the financials.

## Sung-Min Park

Thanks, Jamal. As Jamal mentioned, second quarter revenue was $684 million. Non-GAAP gross margin was 58.3%, up 120 basis points from the prior year and 40 basis points sequentially, driven by favorable mix and better factory utilization. Non-GAAP operating expenses were $247 million, or 36.1% of revenue. Non-GAAP operating margin was 22.2%, an improvement of 210 basis points year over year.

Non-GAAP net income was $172 million, or $1.38 per diluted share, based on 124.6 million diluted shares. GAAP net income was $131 million, or $1.05 per share.

Turning to the balance sheet. We ended the quarter with $1.84 billion in cash and investments and $1.10 billion of debt. Cash flow from operations was $198 million, and free cash flow was $151 million. Inventory days were 118, down from 131 last quarter. We repurchased 1.9 million shares for $104 million and paid a dividend of $0.20 per share.

Now for guidance. For the third quarter, we expect revenue in the range of $705 million to $735 million, non-GAAP gross margin of 58% to 59%, non-GAAP operating expenses of approximately $252 million, and non-GAAP earnings per share of $1.44 to $1.56. We expect a non-GAAP tax rate of 14%. With that, operator, we'll open the line for questions.

## Operator

Thank you. [Operator Instructions] Our first question comes from Rebecca Thornbury of Calder Hayes Research. Please go ahead.

## Rebecca Thornbury -- Calder Hayes Research

Hi, thanks for taking my question. Congrats on the quarter. Jamal, on the 1.6-terabit driver, can you give us a sense of how large you think that product could be in fiscal 2027, and whether the margins are accretive to the corporate average?

## Jamal R. Idowu

Thanks, Rebecca. We're not breaking out the product, but I'd say that data center interconnect, which was about 14% of revenue this quarter, could approach 20% of revenue by the end of next fiscal year. The new driver carries a higher average selling price and, at scale, gross margins that are modestly above the corporate average. In the first year, yields are still ramping, so I would call it neutral to margins.

## Rebecca Thornbury -- Calder Hayes Research

Great. And a quick follow-up for Sung-Min on opex. Should we think about that $252 million as the run rate?

## Sung-Min Park

It's a reasonable run rate for the next two quarters. We'll add some headcount in design and applications engineering, but we're being disciplined. We still expect opex to grow slower than revenue for the full year.

## Operator

Our next question comes from Michael Ostrowski of Pelican Rock Securities. Please go ahead.

## Michael Ostrowski -- Pelican Rock Securities

Thanks. I wanted to ask about tariffs and the supply chain. Have you changed anything about where you're assembling and testing parts, and is any of the third-quarter guidance dependent on a tariff exemption?

## Jamal R. Idowu

Good question, Michael. Roughly 70% of our back-end assembly and test is in Malaysia and the Philippines, and about 15% is in Mexico. We've qualified a second site in Malaysia that comes online in the third quarter. Our guidance assumes the tariff regime as it exists today and does not assume any new exemptions. We've built about two months of safety stock for the most exposed products, which is reflected in the inventory number Sung-Min mentioned.

## Michael Ostrowski -- Pelican Rock Securities

Got it. Thank you.

## Operator

Our next question comes from Anjali Deshpande of Larkin & Vale Equity Research. Please go ahead.

## Anjali Deshpande -- Larkin & Vale Equity Research

Hi, thanks. On automotive, you said 19 platforms. What does the design-win pipeline look like, and what's the revenue conversion timing?

## Jamal R. Idowu

The pipeline is about $2.6 billion of lifetime revenue, up from $2.1 billion a year ago. Typical conversion from design win to meaningful revenue is 18 to 30 months for automotive, so the platforms we won in 2024 are ramping now, and we see a very visible ramp in fiscal 2027.

## Anjali Deshpande -- Larkin & Vale Equity Research

Thank you.

Thank you all for joining us. We're pleased with our momentum, and we look forward to updating you next quarter. Have a great evening.
