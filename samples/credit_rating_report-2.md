HARLOW MUNICIPAL RATINGS SERVICE

NEW ISSUE: CITY OF RIVERBEND, OHIO ASSIGNED Aa2 TO GENERAL OBLIGATION BONDS; OUTLOOK STABLE

October 6, 2026

Rating Action

Harlow Municipal Ratings Service has assigned an Aa2 rating to the City of Riverbend, Ohio's $38.5 million General Obligation Bonds, Series 2026 (Unlimited Tax). The city's outstanding general obligation debt, totaling $214.7 million after this issuance, carries an Aa2 rating, which has been affirmed. The outlook is stable.

Proceeds of the Series 2026 Bonds will fund street reconstruction, a fire station replacement and improvements to the municipal water treatment plant. The bonds are secured by the city's full faith and credit and its pledge to levy ad valorem property taxes, unlimited as to rate and amount, on all taxable property within the city.

Credit Highlights

The Aa2 reflects the city's large and diverse tax base, strong financial position, with general fund reserves of 24.6% of revenues at fiscal year-end 2025, and a moderately high long-term liability burden. The tax base grew 5.8% in fiscal 2026 to $6.4 billion in full value, driven by industrial expansion on the south side and residential construction. Resident income is above average at 112% of the U.S. median.

Offsetting these strengths is a pension liability that, combined with debt and other post-employment benefits, equals 2.9x operating revenues, well above the Aa-category median of 2.1x, and a limited ability to raise additional revenue because of state caps on the city income tax rate.

Rating Outlook

The stable outlook reflects our expectation that the city will maintain reserves above 20% of revenues and that debt issuance will remain in line with the five-year capital plan.

Factors That Could Lead to an Upgrade
- Sustained growth in the tax base combined with a reduction in the long-term liability ratio below 2.0x revenues
- Reserves sustained above 30% of revenues

Factors That Could Lead to a Downgrade
- Structural deficits that cause fund balance to fall below 15% of revenues
- Significant growth in leverage or pension burden
- Loss of the largest employer, which accounts for 8% of income tax receipts
