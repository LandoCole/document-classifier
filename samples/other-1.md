# Fairlane Foods lifts full-year forecast as snack sales offset weak frozen category

By Teresa Vandermolen, Business Correspondent
Updated October 20, 2026, 9:42 a.m. ET

CHICAGO -- Fairlane Foods Holdings raised its profit forecast for the year on Tuesday after third-quarter earnings topped Wall Street's expectations, helped by price increases on its pretzel and granola lines and a drop in ingredient costs.

Shares of the Chicago-based packaged food maker jumped 6.3% in morning trading to $41.15, their biggest one-day gain since February, before settling up 4.8% at midday.

The company said net income for the quarter ended September 30 rose to $142.7 million, or $0.98 a share, from $118.2 million, or $0.80 a share, a year earlier. Analysts polled by a financial data provider had expected earnings of $0.91 a share. Revenue rose 3.2% to $2.46 billion, slightly ahead of the $2.43 billion forecast.

"Consumers are trading down in some categories but they are not giving up their snacks," Chief Executive Alicia Brandvold said in an interview. "We saw volume growth in the mid-single digits in snacks and that gave us room to be disciplined on promotions."

The company now expects full-year adjusted earnings of $3.85 to $3.95 a share, up from a prior range of $3.70 to $3.85. It also lifted its organic sales growth forecast to about 2.5% from a range of 1% to 2%.

Not everything was rosy. Sales in the frozen meals division, which accounts for about a quarter of revenue, fell 4.1% as shoppers shifted toward fresh prepared options at grocery stores. The company said it would close a plant in Tulsa, Oklahoma, next spring, affecting 310 workers, and take a charge of about $55 million.

"Frozen remains the soft spot," said Raymond Ellery, an analyst at the research firm Brookhaven Partners. "But the margin story in snacks is strong enough to carry the quarter, and I think management has been smart about not chasing volume."

Gross margin expanded 140 basis points to 33.6%. Fairlane said commodity costs for wheat and cocoa were lower than a year ago, while labor and freight costs continued to climb.

The company also announced a quarterly dividend of $0.34 a share, payable December 5 to shareholders of record on November 14, and said it bought back $75 million of stock during the quarter.

Fairlane's stock has risen about 11% this year, compared with a gain of 8% for a broader index of food producers.
